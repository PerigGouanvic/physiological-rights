# Prompt Perplexity Deep Research — choline, TMAO, dose thérapeutique

**Usage** : copier tout ce qui est dans le bloc ci-dessous dans une session Perplexity Deep Research (Sonar Reasoning Pro ou équivalent). Coller tel quel, sans modification. Le rapport reviendra dans ce dossier `_inbox/choline-tmao/`.

---

```
I am preparing an evidence audit on the strength of the trimethylamine-N-oxide (TMAO) causal-in-cardiovascular-disease thesis, as it relates to therapeutic choline supplementation. I need a rigorous, source-anchored deep research report. Do not try to reach a consensus verdict where none exists in the literature; when the evidence is contested, present both sides with representative citations and flag the disagreement explicitly.

Context you must accept as framing (not as claims to defend):

1. Choline is a conditionally essential nutrient. Deficiency has documented consequences on hepatic steatosis, muscle damage, and (prenatally) neurodevelopment (Zeisel; Caudill 2018 FASEB J).
2. Therapeutic doses of choline used in the literature range from 500 to 2000 mg/day depending on the indication, with prenatal RCTs testing up to 930 mg/day.
3. Since Wang et al. 2011 NEJM, a substantial line of work led by Stanley Hazen's group has argued that gut microbial conversion of dietary choline, phosphatidylcholine, and L-carnitine into trimethylamine (TMA), oxidized in the liver by FMO3 into TMAO, causally promotes atherosclerosis.
4. A commercial TMAO plasma test was developed and commercialized by Cleveland HeartLab (later acquired by Quest Diagnostics), with patents involving Hazen. This commercial pathway coincides temporally with the causal claim.
5. The dominant dietary source of preformed TMAO in humans is fish (osmolyte in fish tissue), yet fish consumption is broadly cardioprotective in the literature. This is the "fish paradox" referenced by Cho and Caudill 2017 among others.

I want the report structured in the following sections. For every factual claim, provide DOI, PMID, or URL. Where the source is a review, prefer to also cite the primary studies the review draws on. Flag any relevant conflict of interest of the authors.

SECTION 1 — Dose-response of choline intake on plasma TMA and TMAO in humans

- What is the quantitative dose-response between oral choline (all forms) and post-prandial or steady-state plasma TMAO in healthy adults?
- How does the response differ between choline bitartrate, phosphatidylcholine (lecithin), alpha-glycerylphosphorylcholine (alpha-GPC), and citicoline (CDP-choline)?
- What is the individual variability (high-converter vs low-converter phenotype)? Which microbiota taxa are implicated?
- Cite the key intervention studies with sample size, dose, form, and measured endpoint.

SECTION 2 — The fish paradox and its resolution attempts

- Confirm the quantitative claim that fish is the dominant dietary source of preformed TMAO in typical Western diets. Cite Landfald 2017, Cho and Caudill 2017, and any updated work.
- What have researchers proposed to reconcile fish as a major TMAO source with fish as a cardioprotective food?
- Are there human intervention studies comparing plasma TMAO after fish vs after eggs or red meat at matched precursor load?
- Has the "TMAO from fish is different" argument been substantiated mechanistically, or is it ad hoc?

SECTION 3 — Trimethylaminuria (fish odor syndrome, FMO3 loss-of-function)

- Are there published cohort or case-series data on cardiovascular outcomes in patients with severe FMO3 deficiency?
- These patients have high circulating TMA and low TMAO. If TMAO is causally atherogenic, they should show reduced cardiovascular risk. Has this been tested?
- If no such data exist, say so and explain why (sample size, registry limitations).

SECTION 4 — Mendelian randomization on FMO3 variants and on TMAO

- Summarize all Mendelian randomization studies published on FMO3 variants and cardiovascular outcomes.
- Summarize MR studies using genetic instruments for TMAO itself.
- Do the MR studies replicate the observational Hazen signal? Where do they diverge, and how do proponents interpret the divergence?

SECTION 5 — Renal confounding

- How does renal function affect plasma TMAO?
- In the observational cohorts that established the TMAO-cardiovascular mortality association (Tang, Wang, Hazen, and independent replications), what adjustment was made for eGFR or albuminuria?
- Are there cohort analyses restricted to participants with normal renal function that preserve the signal? Are there any that lose it?
- What is the current position of nephrology researchers (e.g., Meijers, Vanholder, Missailidis) on the reverse-causation question?

SECTION 6 — Alpha-GPC and CDP-choline: TMAO burden

- Direct measurements of plasma TMA or TMAO following alpha-GPC or citicoline supplementation in humans.
- Do these forms bypass gut microbial TMA production to a clinically meaningful degree?
- Are there long-term safety data for these forms specifically?

SECTION 7 — Sparing cofactors

- Documentation on the reduction of choline requirement by betaine, folate, and vitamin B12 (via homocysteine remethylation).
- Are there interventional studies where a betaine or folate co-supplement reduces plasma TMAO for a given choline intake?
- PEMT SNP data: how much does the PEMT rs7946 variant affect choline requirement and TMAO response?

SECTION 8 — Official recommendations (2020 to 2026)

- What are the current Adequate Intake and Tolerable Upper Intake Level for choline per the IOM/NASEM, EFSA, WHO/FAO, and any national body of note?
- Do these bodies acknowledge the TMAO controversy in their rationales? If yes, cite the wording; if no, note the absence.
- Have any professional societies (AHA, ESC, ACC) issued position statements on TMAO as a clinical target?

SECTION 9 — Commercialization of the TMAO test

- Timeline of the TMAO plasma test: research publication, patent filings, launch by Cleveland HeartLab, acquisition by Quest Diagnostics.
- Which patents name Stanley Hazen or the Cleveland Clinic as inventors or assignees? Provide patent numbers and dates.
- Marketing materials, clinical decision guides, or media pieces that recommend restricting choline, eggs, or red meat on the basis of a TMAO test. Do these materials symmetrically warn about fish consumption?
- Any documented conflicts of interest disclosures in the primary Hazen-lab publications.

SECTION 10 — Known unknowns

- Explicitly list the studies that would settle the causal question but have not been done. Prioritize by feasibility.
- Identify at least three research groups currently working on the mechanistic or epidemiological side of TMAO who are independent of the Hazen lab.

FORMAT REQUIREMENTS

- Do not use em dashes.
- Cite every factual claim with DOI, PMID, or URL immediately after the claim.
- Do not summarize; be specific. Numbers, doses, sample sizes, effect estimates with confidence intervals where available.
- Where the evidence is genuinely contested, use language like "proponents argue X (citation); critics argue Y (citation)". Do not force a synthesis.
- If a question cannot be answered from the available literature, state that explicitly rather than filling with speculation.
- Do not defer to consensus statements when the primary literature they summarize is thin or contested. The point of this audit is to expose the strength or weakness of the underlying evidence, not to rehearse what expert bodies currently say.
```
