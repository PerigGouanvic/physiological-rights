# Christiane Druml

Jurist, bioethicist and institutional builder. Born 4 May 1955 in Vienna. Dr. iur., University of Vienna, 1978, trained under **Felix Ermacora**, the Austrian jurist who served as UN Special Rapporteur on human rights, member of the UN Sub-Commission on Prevention of Discrimination and Protection of Minorities, and one of the principal European figures of the post-1945 international-human-rights generation. That filiation matters: Druml is not a physician who moved into ethics but a lawyer trained in the Ermacora tradition who moved into medical institutions and, from there, into the ethics apparatus of European clinical nutrition.

## The Austrian institutional geography

Druml holds, or has held, an unusually dense set of positions:

- **1992-2011**: Director of the Medical Ethics Committee of the Medical University of Vienna. Twenty years running the research-ethics review body of what is the largest medical faculty in the German-speaking world.
- **Since 2007**: Chair of the Bioethikkommission beim Bundeskanzleramt (Austrian Bioethics Commission at the Federal Chancellery). This is the national body that advises the Austrian government on all bioethical questions of legislative significance.
- **2008-2016**: Member of the International Bioethics Committee (IBC) of UNESCO. This is the position through which she is connected to the same UNESCO-bioethics network in which Diana Cárdenas trained.
- **Since 2015**: Director of the Josephinum, the museum, historical collection and ethics-teaching institution of the Medical University of Vienna, housed in the eighteenth-century building on Währinger Strasse that Emperor Joseph II built as the imperial military surgical academy.
- **Since 2016**: UNESCO Chair on Bioethics at MedUni Wien (renewed through 2028). One of very few UNESCO chairs held by a jurist rather than a physician.
- **Since April 2016**: Deputy Director of the Ludwig Boltzmann Institute for Rare and Undiagnosed Diseases (LBI-RUD).

## The assisted-suicide file

Druml is one of the principal architects of the Austrian regime on assisted suicide. The Bioethikkommission she chairs produced, in 2015, the report *Sterben in Würde* (Dying in Dignity) that framed the subsequent Constitutional Court ruling. The **Sterbeverfügungsgesetz** of 2022, which regulates assisted suicide in Austria, traces directly to that report. This is not incidental to the nutritional-care file: her authority in end-of-life ethics is what makes her the natural last author of ESPEN's guidelines on ethical aspects of nutrition and hydration, given how much of the clinical territory is end-of-life decision-making around artificial feeding and withdrawal.

## The ESPEN 2016 guideline

**Druml C. (first author), Ballmer P.E., Druml W., Oehmichen F., Shenkin A., Singer P., Soeters P., Weimann A., Bischoff S.C.**, *ESPEN Guideline on Ethical Aspects of Artificial Nutrition and Hydration*, *Clinical Nutrition* 2016;35(3):545-556. [DOI 10.1016/j.clnu.2016.02.006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clnu.2016.02.006).

This is her entry into the ESPEN institutional structure. The 2016 guideline operates within the Beauchamp-Childress principlist framework (autonomy, beneficence, non-maleficence, justice) and does not deploy human-rights vocabulary. It is a classical Anglo-American bioethics text, competent and cautious, focused on decision-making around artificial nutrition and hydration in end-of-life care and in patients unable to consent.

**Wilfred Druml** (co-author on that 2016 guideline) is a separate person: nephrologist at MedUni Wien, specialist in acute kidney injury and nutritional support of ICU patients, and, by the concordance of family name and affiliation, likely her spouse. He is not the bioethicist. When later texts (including some of the derivative literature on the 2026 guideline) cite "Druml" as last author, the reference is to Christiane, not to Wilfred.

## The ESPEN 2026 practical guideline

**Cárdenas D. (first author), Burgos R., Barazzoni R., Cederholm T., Correia M.I.T.D., Gramlich L., Hardy G., Ljungqvist O., Ochoa J.B., Wischmeyer P.E., Druml C. (last author)**, *ESPEN Practical Guideline on Ethical Aspects of Medical Nutrition Therapy*, *Clinical Nutrition*, 1 May 2026. [DOI 10.1016/j.clnu.2026.106622](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clnu.2026.106622).

The 2026 guideline is the update of the 2016 one, but with the authorship pole reversed. Cárdenas is now first author; Druml keeps the anchoring last-author position. The Beauchamp-Childress principlist framework is retained, but the human-right-to-nutritional-care formulation from the [Vienna Declaration](/currents/vienna-declaration-nutritional-care/) is added as normative foundation. Druml's presence in the last-author slot is what gives the guideline its ESPEN institutional legitimacy in the German-speaking clinical world; Cárdenas in the first-author slot is what gives it its philosophical and Latin American human-rights spine. The two authorship poles are the two hinges of the argument.

## The Josephinum and the historical dimension

The Josephinum is not a routine museum. It houses the Florentine anatomical wax collection commissioned by Joseph II in 1785, the historical library of Vienna medicine, and the ethics teaching of the medical faculty. Under Druml's direction it has become one of the European sites where the history-of-medicine argument (what medicine has been and has done, including its capacity for institutional violence) is systematically put in dialogue with the ethics teaching of active physicians. The 2018 reopening after refurbishment placed it, symbolically, at the centre of the MedUni Wien ethics curriculum.

## Why she matters here

Three points.

**First**, on the passport. Druml is the person through whom the human-rights argument on nutritional care enters the ESPEN guideline machinery, and through it the European clinical practice standard. Cárdenas wrote the argument; Druml supplies the ESPEN and MedUni Wien institutional legitimacy that lets the argument sit in a prescriptive text rather than a declaratory one. Any productive engagement with the Vienna and 2026 texts routes through her.

**Second**, on the jurist's contribution. Because she is a jurist trained under Ermacora rather than a physician, Druml carries into ESPEN a background that no other member of the working group has: direct familiarity with the international-human-rights-law apparatus, with UNESCO bioethics, and with the institutional culture in which a right is not a slogan but an operational category. That is why, in an authorship structure that is otherwise dominated by clinicians and dietitians, her position at the anchoring last-author slot is not honorific but load-bearing.

**Third**, on the Vienna anchor. That the Vienna Declaration was signed in Vienna in September 2022 and that the ESPEN Practical Guideline on Ethical Aspects of Medical Nutrition Therapy has its anchoring author in Vienna is not a coincidence. It is the concentration, in one city and around one institution (the Josephinum and MedUni Wien) of the ethics apparatus that a rights-based reading of clinical nutrition needs. The physiological-rights argument developed here needs a European institutional home; the Josephinum is, in 2026, the closest thing to it.
