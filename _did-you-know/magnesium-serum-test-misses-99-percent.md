# Did you know…

**Less than 1% of your body's magnesium circulates in the blood. The remaining 99% is locked inside cells, bones, and muscles.** The standard serum magnesium test measures the 1%, and its reference range is calibrated on a magnesium-depleted population. A person can be profoundly magnesium-deficient at the tissue level and receive a normal serum result.

## The claim, in the words of the international magnesium research network

The MaGNet network is an international consortium of magnesium researchers whose 2022 recommendation in *Advances in Nutrition* is the most direct statement on record about the failure of the standard test:

> *"Widely varying serum magnesium reference ranges render our use of this important medical tool imprecise, minimizing impacts of low magnesium status or hypomagnesemia as a marker of disease risk."*
>
> — MaGNet Network, *Advances in Nutrition*, 2022

Their clinical review had earlier established, in the same field, the more fundamental point:

> *"An individual may be profoundly deficient in total body or intracellular Mg required for various cellular biochemical processes yet have a SMC [serum magnesium concentration] within normal range."*
>
> — MaGNet clinical review

## What the numbers actually show

In one study of hypertensive patients, **80% had magnesium depletion confirmed by a loading test** even though their blood magnesium was normal or elevated. Among patients hospitalized in intensive care units, **half are deficient in intracellular magnesium despite a normal serum result**. A 2010 analysis found that only **10% of cases of hypomagnesaemia** were detected at the physician's request; the remaining 90% were found incidentally during routine tests. Physicians are not even looking.

## The recommendation nobody has adopted

MaGNet recommends raising the minimum threshold from the usual 0.70-0.75 mmol/L to **0.85 mmol/L**. No major laboratory has updated its printouts. The test that misses the tissue continues to reassure the patient at values that, on a properly calibrated scale, would already flag a functional deficit.
