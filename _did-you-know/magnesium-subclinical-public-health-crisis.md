# Did you know…

**Subclinical magnesium deficiency is not a fringe supplement-industry claim. It is the subject of a peer-reviewed statement in a BMJ journal that names it a public health crisis.** The paper was published in 2018. In 2025, a global review confirmed that 2.4 billion people, 31% of the world's population, still do not meet the recommended magnesium intake.

## The claim, in the words of a peer-reviewed BMJ paper

James DiNicolantonio, cardiovascular research scientist at Saint Luke's Mid America Heart Institute, published in *Open Heart* (BMJ) in 2018 a paper whose title carries the conclusion: *"Subclinical magnesium deficiency: a principal driver of cardiovascular disease and a public health crisis."* The core sentence of the paper reads:

> *"Subclinical magnesium deficiency increases the risk of numerous types of cardiovascular disease, costs nations around the world an incalculable amount of healthcare costs and suffering, and should be considered a public health crisis."*
>
> — DiNicolantonio et al., *Open Heart* (BMJ), 2018

The same authors, in the same paper, wrote the diagnostic diagnosis that has been ignored by every reference laboratory that continues to print the old ranges:

> *"Because serum magnesium does not reflect intracellular magnesium, the latter making up more than 99% of total body magnesium, most cases of magnesium deficiency are undiagnosed."*
>
> — DiNicolantonio et al., *Open Heart* (BMJ), 2018

## What the cost estimate looks like

In the United States, one analyst calculated that the cost of inaction on magnesium deficiency exceeds **$86 billion per year**. In Israel, a Taub Center study estimated that simply adding magnesium to desalinated water could generate savings of **83 to 253 million shekels per year** by reducing type 2 diabetes and ischaemic stroke. Magnesium supplementation costs a few cents per day.

## Why nothing has moved

The 2018 paper called it a public health crisis. No national screening policy has been created. In 2025, the global review documented 2.4 billion people short of the recommended intake. The official public-health message across most jurisdictions remains: eat a balanced diet.
