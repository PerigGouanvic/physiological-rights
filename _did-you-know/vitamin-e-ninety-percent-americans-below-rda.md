# Did you know…

**More than 90% of Americans do not consume enough vitamin E to meet the estimated average requirement, and the standard blood test cannot detect the deficit.** This is not a fringe claim. It comes from Maret Traber, the researcher who helped set the reference in the first place.

## The claim, in the words of a member of the Institute of Medicine panel

Maret Traber directs vitamin E research at the Linus Pauling Institute at Oregon State University. She sat on the Institute of Medicine panel that fixed the Recommended Dietary Allowance for vitamin E at 15 mg/day of alpha-tocopherol. In *Advances in Nutrition* in 2014, she reported the two-part finding that a public-health system rooted in her own reference has not integrated:

> *"It is estimated that >90% of Americans do not consume sufficient dietary vitamin E, as α-tocopherol, to meet estimated average requirements. [...] Seemingly, adequacy of human vitamin E status cannot be assessed from circulating α-tocopherol concentrations."*
>
> — Maret Traber, *Advances in Nutrition*, 2014

## The full family, not the isolated form

There are eight members of the vitamin E family: four tocopherols (alpha, beta, gamma, delta) and four tocotrienols. Most supplements and most research protocols focus exclusively on alpha-tocopherol. Yet, as Packer, Weber, and Rimbach wrote in *Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences* in 2006:

> *"Current developments in vitamin E research clearly indicate that members of the vitamin E family are not redundant with respect to their biological functions."*
>
> — Packer, Weber, Rimbach, *Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences*, 2006

Alpha-tocopherol taken alone actively depletes gamma-tocopherol from the body, driving it to 30-50% of normal levels. And no laboratory in ordinary use measures tocotrienols.

## What has been explored, and what has not

Vitamin E is not a nutrient that science has fully explored. It is a nutrient of which science has explored roughly one eighth of the territory, concluded on the basis of that partial exploration that the rest did not exist, and stopped measuring.
