# Right to Adequate Vitamin C Status

> *"Man, apes, guinea pigs, an Indian fruit-eating bat, and some birds share a loss of ability to synthesize ascorbic acid. For other mammals, ascorbic acid is not a vitamin at all. It is a biochemical product that the body manufactures in large quantities as it needs."*
> — Irwin Stone, *The Healing Factor*, 1972

## The four dose levels

Discussion of vitamin C intake has been systematically fogged by collapsing four biologically distinct dose regimes into a single argument about "megadoses." They are not the same thing.

1. **Anti-scorbutic doses (RDA).** Roughly 75 to 200 mg per day across jurisdictions. The ceiling, not the floor. Designed to prevent overt scurvy, not to optimize physiology. Distributes subclinical scurvy across tissues while maintaining a "normal" serum reading.
2. **Great-ape doses.** 3 to 5 grams per day. What our closest living primate relatives actually consume in the wild, and by inference what our evolutionary line likely received. 10 to 20 times RDA. Documented in the primatological literature; see Katharine Milton's 1999 article in *Nutrition* on the nutritional characteristics of wild primate foods.
3. **Mammalian doses.** The equivalent of 10 to 20 grams per day in humans, matching what ascorbate-synthesizing mammals produce endogenously in baseline conditions. Rises sharply under stress, infection, trauma, or toxic insult. Documented quantitatively by I. B. Chatterjee (*Science*, 1973) in the comparative biosynthesis literature. This is the level Linus Pauling pointed to when he remarked that he would trust the biochemistry of a goat over the opinion of a doctor.
4. **Therapeutic doses.** Grams to decagrams, oral or intravenous, used to reach a diseased tissue with a pharmacologically active concentration. Cathcart's bowel-tolerance titration, Hoffer and Riordan's oncology work, Padayatty's IV pharmacokinetics all operate here. This is the register in which conventional medicine itself routinely operates when it administers 1 g of amoxicillin or 500 mg of metformin.

The distinction matters. Levels 1 to 3 are not "megadoses." Level 1 is a regulatory floor, level 2 is a species-typical intake, level 3 is the mammalian baseline. Only level 4 is pharmacological, and there the human species is not doing anything its own medicine does not already do with every prescription it writes.

A consequence that recurs across tissues: a person can carry a "normal" serum ascorbate while running what might be called **scurvy of the elbow, scurvy of the knee, scurvy of the brain, scurvy of the liver, scurvy of the kidney**, scurvy of any specific tissue or organelle whose local ascorbate concentration has collapsed. Serum ascorbate is the water in the tap. Tissue ascorbate is the pool. The RDA fills the tap.

## The biochemical catalogue

The following inventory lists the enzymes and processes in which vitamin C participates as cofactor, cosubstrate, or regenerator. Nothing here is controversial at the biochemistry level; every entry is standard textbook material. What is unusual is to see them all in one place, because the picture they draw of what a vitamin-C-sufficient body can do is unfamiliar even to those who prescribe its deficit.

### Family 1. Iron(II) / 2-oxoglutarate-dependent dioxygenases

These enzymes use iron(II), molecular oxygen, 2-oxoglutarate, and ascorbate. Ascorbate's role is to reduce iron that has escaped the catalytic cycle in its ferric form back to the ferrous form that permits catalysis. Without sufficient ascorbate, the enzymes progressively stall.

| Enzyme | Reaction | Biological substrate | Critical tissues | Functional consequence of deficit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Prolyl 4-hydroxylase** (collagen P4H) | Hydroxylation of proline in collagen | Collagen types I–V | Skin, tendon, bone, vessel wall, gum | Collagen triple helix unstable: capillary fragility, gum bleeding, tendon failure, poor wound healing. The classical scurvy phenotype. |
| **Prolyl 3-hydroxylase** | 3-hydroxylation of proline in collagen | Collagen | Bone, cartilage | Bone fragility (osteogenesis imperfecta type VIII when mutated). |
| **Lysyl hydroxylase** (PLOD1–3) | Hydroxylation of lysine in collagen | Collagen | Skin, bone, vessel | Crosslink defects; kyphoscoliotic Ehlers-Danlos when mutated. |
| **γ-Butyrobetaine hydroxylase** (BBOX1) | Final step of carnitine biosynthesis | γ-butyrobetaine → L-carnitine | Liver, kidney | Reduced carnitine: impaired β-oxidation of long-chain fatty acids, muscular fatigue, cardiac energy deficit. |
| **Trimethyllysine hydroxylase** (TMLHE) | First step of carnitine biosynthesis | ε-N-trimethyllysine | Liver, kidney, muscle | As above, with autism-spectrum associations in TMLHE deficiency. |
| **HIF prolyl hydroxylases** (PHD1, PHD2, PHD3 / EGLN1–3) | Hydroxylation of HIF-α under normoxia, targeting it for degradation | HIF-1α, HIF-2α | All tissues (oxygen sensing) | Dysregulated hypoxic response: altered erythropoiesis, angiogenesis, metabolic switching. Pseudohypoxia under normal oxygen. |
| **FIH** (factor inhibiting HIF) | Asparagine hydroxylation of HIF-α | HIF-α C-terminal transactivation domain | All tissues | Fine-tuning of HIF transcriptional activity. |
| **TET1, TET2, TET3** | Oxidation of 5-methylcytosine in DNA (5mC → 5hmC → 5fC → 5caC) | Genomic DNA | All cells, especially hematopoietic, neural | Impaired active DNA demethylation: epigenetic drift, altered hematopoiesis (TET2 is a major clonal hematopoiesis locus), neurodevelopmental effects. |
| **JmjC histone demethylases (KDMs)** | Demethylation of lysine residues on histones | Histones H3, H4 | All cells | Impaired transcriptional regulation; chromatin state dysregulation. |
| **AlkB homologs (ALKBH1–8, FTO)** | Oxidative demethylation of alkylated DNA and RNA | DNA/RNA bases | All cells | Compromised repair of alkylation damage; altered RNA modification (m6A via FTO). |
| **Asparaginyl/aspartyl β-hydroxylase** (ASPH) | Hydroxylation of EGF domains | Coagulation factors (VII, IX, X), Notch, others | Liver, developing tissues | Impaired coagulation factor maturation; developmental signaling effects. |

### Family 2. Copper-dependent monooxygenases using ascorbate as reductant

These enzymes use copper and molecular oxygen. Ascorbate donates electrons to regenerate the catalytic copper. Without sufficient ascorbate, copper stalls in its oxidized form and catalysis stops.

| Enzyme | Reaction | Biological substrate | Critical tissues | Functional consequence of deficit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Dopamine β-hydroxylase (DBH)** | Dopamine → norepinephrine | Dopamine | Adrenal medulla, sympathetic neurons, locus coeruleus | Impaired synthesis of norepinephrine: fatigue, orthostatic intolerance, blunted stress response, cognitive slowing, mood effects. |
| **Peptidyl-glycine α-amidating monooxygenase (PAM)** | C-terminal α-amidation of peptide hormones (requires glycine-extended precursor; PAM = PHM activity, followed by PAL lyase activity) | β-endorphin and enkephalin precursors (POMC products), oxytocin, vasopressin, substance P, cholecystokinin (CCK), gastrin, calcitonin, α-MSH, NPY, VIP, and many others | Hypothalamus, pituitary, gut, pancreas, adrenal medulla | Peptide hormones and neuropeptides released in a non-amidated, largely inactive form. Direct mechanistic link to dysregulation of the endogenous opioid system (hence the association with addiction biology), to impaired attachment and anxiolysis (oxytocin), to altered appetite and satiety (CCK, NPY), to disturbed pain signaling (substance P). |
| **PAL** (peptidyl-α-hydroxyglycine α-amidating lyase) | Second catalytic domain of the PAM bifunctional enzyme; completes amidation | Same substrates as PHM | Same tissues | Same consequences as PHM; PAL is intrinsically paired with PHM in the mammalian PAM gene. |
| **Tyrosinase** (in the ascorbate-modulated pigmentation pathway; ascorbate acts as a downregulator by reducing dopaquinone back to DOPA, moderating melanogenesis) | Tyrosine → DOPA → dopaquinone | Tyrosine | Skin, hair, eye (retinal pigment epithelium) | Dysregulated melanogenesis; not a classical deficiency consequence but a documented modulatory link. |

### Family 3. Regeneration of other antioxidants

Vitamin C does not merely scavenge; it regenerates other reduced species that have been consumed scavenging. This makes it the hub of a redox network, not a point reducer.

- **α-Tocopherol (vitamin E) regeneration.** The tocopheroxyl radical, produced when vitamin E has neutralized a lipid peroxyl radical in the membrane, is reduced back to α-tocopherol by ascorbate at the aqueous–lipid interface. Without ascorbate, vitamin E is consumed and lipid peroxidation chains propagate.
- **Glutathione (GSH) regeneration.** Ascorbate can reduce glutathione disulfide (GSSG) back to GSH, both directly and via coupled enzyme systems. The ascorbate–glutathione cycle is central in plants and partially active in mammals.
- **Tetrahydrobiopterin (BH4) regeneration.** Ascorbate maintains BH4 in its reduced form, required for nitric oxide synthases and for aromatic amino acid hydroxylases (phenylalanine, tyrosine, tryptophan hydroxylases). This connects ascorbate indirectly to serotonin synthesis (via TPH) and nitric oxide production (via NOS), linking it to endothelial function, mood, and sleep regulation.

### Family 4. Non-enzymatic actions

- **Direct radical scavenging.** Ascorbate neutralizes superoxide, hydroxyl, peroxyl, singlet oxygen, and nitrogen-centered radicals. The brain in particular maintains ascorbate concentrations in the millimolar range (10 to 100 times plasma), consistent with a redox-buffer role at cellular level.
- **Ferric-to-ferrous iron reduction.** Ascorbate in the duodenal lumen reduces dietary non-heme iron (Fe³⁺) to the absorbable Fe²⁺ form, directly enhancing iron uptake. This is why ascorbate deficiency promotes iron-deficiency anemia even with adequate dietary iron, and why high-dose ascorbate complicates iron overload states.
- **Nitrosamine neutralization.** Ascorbate reacts with nitrosating agents in the stomach, reducing the formation of carcinogenic N-nitroso compounds from dietary nitrites and amines. Mechanistically well established; epidemiologically contested.
- **Neutrophil and leukocyte function.** Phagocytes concentrate ascorbate by two orders of magnitude above plasma. During the oxidative burst, intraleukocyte ascorbate protects the cell from its own output and enables apoptosis of spent neutrophils, resolving inflammation rather than letting it drift.

## Why cataloguing matters

Reading the inventory in one sitting makes a point that no single entry can make alone. A molecule that participates in collagen synthesis, carnitine synthesis, norepinephrine synthesis, neuropeptide activation, DNA demethylation, histone demethylation, DNA repair, oxygen sensing, vitamin E regeneration, glutathione regeneration, tetrahydrobiopterin regeneration, iron absorption, and millimolar neural redox buffering is not a vitamin in the ordinary English sense. It is a general-purpose biochemical infrastructure that the human body, uniquely among most mammals, has to acquire from outside because the species-specific GULO pseudogene prevents us from making our own.

The practical consequence is that a dose level sufficient to prevent overt scurvy (level 1 above) does not come close to supplying this infrastructure. Scurvy of the elbow, scurvy of the brain, scurvy of the kidney, scurvy of the tissue of your choice follows from the same cause: the tap is open, the pool is empty.
