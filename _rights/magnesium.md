# Right to Adequate Magnesium Levels

> *"Subclinical magnesium deficiency increases the risk of numerous types of cardiovascular disease, costs nations around the world an incalculable amount of healthcare costs and suffering, and should be considered a public health crisis."*
> — DiNicolantonio et al., *Open Heart* (BMJ), 2018{% include cite.html id="dinicolantonio-2018" ex="abstract" %}

---

## A century of ignored warnings

The history of magnesium deficiency is the history of a slow-motion scandal. As early as **1926**, magnesium was recognized as an essential nutrient for life.{% include cite.html id="durlach-1988" ex="abstract" %} In **1936**, the US Senate printed as *Senate Document 264* a magazine article by Rex Beach, first published in *Cosmopolitan* and entered into the record by Senator Duncan Fletcher, on the work of Dr Charles Northen. It already raised the alarm: agricultural soils were losing their minerals, and the foods they produced were no longer properly nourishing the population; magnesium appears in it by name, in children "deficient in magnesia".{% include cite.html id="senate-doc-264" ex="soils,magnesia" %} That warning is almost 90 years old. Nothing structural has changed.

In **1971**, Professor **Jean Durlach** (1925–2017), of the Université Pierre et Marie Curie in Paris, organized the first International Symposium on Magnesium in Vittel and founded the **SDRM** (*Société pour le Développement de la Recherche sur le Magnésium*).{% include cite.html id="rayssiguier-2017" ex="obituary" %} In **1980**, Dr **Mildred Seelig** published her monumental work *Magnesium Deficiency in the Pathogenesis of Disease*, in which she meticulously documented the role of magnesium deficiency in cardiovascular, renal, neuromuscular, and immune diseases.{% include cite.html id="seelig-1980-preface" ex="inferential" %}

In **2018**, DiNicolantonio and colleagues published in *Open Heart* (BMJ) a paper whose title says it all: *"Subclinical magnesium deficiency: a principal driver of cardiovascular disease and a public health crisis."*{% include cite.html id="dinicolantonio-2018" ex="abstract" %} In **2024**, a modelling study in *The Lancet Global Health* estimated that **2.4 billion people**, 31% of the world's population, do not get the recommended magnesium intake from their food.{% include cite.html id="passarelli-2024" ex="findings,methods" %} A 2025 global review repeated the figure.{% include cite.html id="zhang-zhao-2025" ex="prevalence" %} A century of warnings. And the world keeps sleeping.

## Why standard blood tests are a trap

### The pool and the tap

To understand the testing problem, imagine the body as a large house with an enormous underground pool (the cells, bones, muscles) and a tiny tap running outside (the blood). The standard blood test, **serum magnesium**, only measures the water from the tap. Yet **less than 1% of total body magnesium** circulates in the blood. The remaining 99% is locked inside cells, bones, and muscles.{% include cite.html id="jahnen-dechent-2012" ex="distribution,testing" %}

Worse still: the body is programmed to maintain a "normal" blood level at all costs. When blood magnesium begins to fall, the organism **draws on its bone and muscle reserves** to refill the tap. Result: a person can be **profoundly deficient** in their cells, bones, and muscles, and yet show a "normal" result on their blood test.{% include cite.html id="rosanoff-2022" ex="clmd" %}

### What the numbers say

Gerry Schwalfenberg, of the University of Alberta, and Stephen Genuis put it plainly in their 2017 review:

> *"[…] an individual may be profoundly deficient in total body or intracellular Mg required for various cellular biochemical processes yet have a SMC [serum magnesium concentration] within normal range."*{% include cite.html id="schwalfenberg-2017" ex="serum" %}

One study of patients treated for hypertension for at least six months showed that **80% of them** abnormally retained a magnesium load, a sign of depletion, even though their serum magnesium was **normal**.{% include cite.html id="seelig-cb-1990" ex="abstract" %} In 104 patients admitted to an intensive cardiac care unit, **53%** had low magnesium inside their blood cells, while only **7.7%** had low serum magnesium.{% include cite.html id="ryzen-1986" ex="abstract" %}

In 1990, Whang and Ryder measured magnesium systematically in 1,033 serum samples sent for electrolyte analysis. Physicians had requested magnesium for only 7% of them, and those requests caught **only 48 of the 487 cases of hypomagnesaemia**, about 10%.{% include cite.html id="whang-1990" ex="abstract" %} The other 90% surfaced only because the researchers tested everything. In other words, physicians are not even looking.

### Real tests exist, but are not used

More reliable tools exist: the **magnesium loading test** (intravenous injection followed by urinary collection), **erythrocyte (red blood cell) magnesium**, **mononuclear cell magnesium**, **muscle biopsy**, and **NMR spectroscopy**. But these tests are more expensive, more complex, and above all, they are **not routinely available** to most clinicians.{% include cite.html id="schwalfenberg-2017" ex="serum" %}{% include cite.html id="workinger-2018" ex="rbc" %} The result is a vicious circle: you cannot diagnose what you are not looking for, with a tool that shows nothing anyway.

## Magnesium: far more than "300 enzymatic reactions"

### The real figure: up to 800

The phrase "magnesium is involved in more than 300 enzymatic reactions" has become a mindlessly repeated mantra. But it is **outdated**. Current enzyme databases show that magnesium is a direct cofactor for **more than 600 enzymes** and may activate **200 more**.{% include cite.html id="souza-2023" ex="enzymes" %} Up to 800 enzymatic systems that depend on this single mineral. A 2012 annotation of the human proteome by Piovesan and colleagues found **3,751 human proteins** with putative magnesium-binding sites.{% include cite.html id="piovesan-2012" ex="results" %}

To put this in perspective: without its enzyme, one step in making the building blocks of DNA and RNA would proceed with a half-life of **78 million years**;{% include cite.html id="radzicka-wolfenden-1995" ex="abstract" %} one step in making haem, the iron-carrying core of haemoglobin, **2.3 billion years**, half the age of the Earth.{% include cite.html id="lewis-wolfenden-2008" ex="abstract" %} Enzymes are the accelerators of all the chemistry of life. Those two happen to work without any metal; but the kinases that transfer phosphate and the polymerases that copy DNA and RNA cannot work without magnesium bound to their nucleotide substrates.{% include cite.html id="souza-2023" ex="atp" %} Magnesium is the key that starts an enormous number of these accelerators.

### ATP: the currency of life's energy

**ATP** (adenosine triphosphate) is the molecule that every cell in the body uses as its "energy currency." Every time a muscle contracts, a neuron transmits a signal, a cell divides, or a protein is manufactured, it is ATP that provides the necessary energy.

Here is the crucial fact that almost no one communicates to the general public: **ATP does not exist in its active form without magnesium**. The ATP molecule is unstable on its own. To function, it must bind to a magnesium ion, forming the **Mg-ATP complex**. It is this complex, not free ATP, that the kinases and ATPases actually bind and use. In practice, nearly every enzymatic reaction that spends ATP has an **absolute requirement for magnesium**.{% include cite.html id="souza-2023" ex="atp" %}{% include cite.html id="kothari-2024" ex="atp" %}

Imagine ATP as a banknote. Without magnesium, this note is not recognized by the cellular "cash registers." It is there, but unusable.

## A staggering inventory of associated diseases

The list of medical conditions linked to magnesium deficiency is so long that it becomes difficult to believe. Yet each of these associations is documented in peer-reviewed scientific literature.

An association is not a causal claim. A magnesium deficit is one etiology among several to explore for each of the conditions below, not the sole one. What the inventory shows is that the routine serum test does not name the deficit when it is present, so the etiology never enters the differential. Each row below draws on the clinical review of Schwalfenberg and Genuis, which grades every indication by level of evidence.{% include cite.html id="schwalfenberg-2017" ex="levelone,cardio,neuro,pregnancy,asthma,stones,eyes,skin,cancer" %}

| System affected | Conditions associated with Mg deficiency |
|---|---|
| Cardiovascular | Hypertension, arrhythmias, sudden death, atherosclerosis, heart failure |
| Metabolic | Type 1 and 2 diabetes, metabolic syndrome, insulin resistance |
| Skeletal | Osteoporosis, fractures, vitamin D-resistant rickets |
| Neurological/mental | Depression, anxiety, migraines, insomnia, ADHD, Alzheimer's, Parkinson's |
| Reproductive | Pre-eclampsia, eclampsia, prematurity, low birth weight |
| Respiratory | Asthma, COPD{% include cite.html id="dinicolantonio-2018" ex="copd" %} |
| Renal | Kidney stones (calcium oxalate) |
| Ocular | Cataracts, glaucoma |
| Dermatological | Atopic dermatitis |
| Oncological | Increased risk of colorectal and breast cancer |

### Cardiovascular disease

In the Nurses' Health Study, women in the highest quartile of plasma magnesium had a **77% lower risk of sudden cardiac death** than those in the lowest quartile; for dietary magnesium, the reduction was 37%.{% include cite.html id="chiuve-2011" ex="abstract" %} A 2013 meta-analysis of 19 prospective cohorts (532,979 participants in total) found a relative risk of cardiovascular events of 0.77 for the highest versus lowest serum magnesium category, and 0.85 for dietary intake.{% include cite.html id="qu-2013" ex="abstract" %}

### Diabetes and metabolic syndrome

Magnesium is involved in insulin signalling and glucose metabolism. An increase of 100 mg/day of magnesium is associated with a **15% reduction in the risk of type 2 diabetes**.{% include cite.html id="larsson-wolk-2007" ex="abstract" %} In one study of 210 diabetic patients, **88.6% consumed less than the recommended intake** of magnesium, and 37.1% were measurably hypomagnesaemic.{% include cite.html id="schwalfenberg-2017" ex="diabetes" %}

### Bone health

Magnesium is a structural component of hydroxyapatite, the primary bone mineral. It is also **essential for the activation of vitamin D**: without sufficient magnesium, vitamin D cannot be converted to its active form, a phenomenon called "magnesium-dependent vitamin D resistance." Children with rickets who did not respond to massive doses of vitamin D have been cured by magnesium supplementation.{% include cite.html id="schwalfenberg-2017" ex="vitd,bone" %}

### Mental health and neurological function

Magnesium is necessary for the conversion of tryptophan to serotonin (the "feel-good" neurotransmitter).{% include cite.html id="schwalfenberg-2017" ex="depression" %} It is also a natural antagonist of NMDA receptors, the same mechanism of action as ketamine, now used as an emergency antidepressant. Sharing a target is not the same as sharing an effect, but the overlap explains why magnesium keeps appearing in depression research.{% include cite.html id="schwalfenberg-2017" ex="nmda,depression" %}

## Health policies facing their own failure

### "Eat a balanced diet": advice that has become hollow

Since 1940, the magnesium content of foods has dropped dramatically. Comparing successive editions of McCance and Widdowson's British food composition tables (1940–2002), David Thomas found that vegetables have lost **24% of their magnesium**, whole milk 21%, cheddar cheese 38%, parmesan 70%.{% include cite.html id="thomas-2007" ex="abstract" %} Refining compounds the loss: white flour retains about a fifth of the wheat's magnesium (**82%** lost), polished rice loses 83%, white sugar 99% (figures compiled by Marier, 1986).{% include cite.html id="marier-1986" ex="abstract" %}{% include cite.html id="workinger-2018" ex="processing" %} Intensive agriculture compounds it again: high-yield harvests export magnesium from the soil, while the standard fertilizers supply nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium, not magnesium.{% include cite.html id="cazzola-2020" ex="fertilizers" %}

To say today "eat a balanced diet" for magnesium is to tell someone to fill a bucket with a tap whose flow has been halved. Even a diet rich in fruits, vegetables, and whole grains **no longer necessarily provides the same magnesium intake as it did 70 years ago**.

### The financial cost of inaction

In Israel, a 2024 Taub Center study (Sadeh, Grotto, Davidovitch and Weinreb) calculated that adding magnesium to desalinated water would save **83 to 188 million shekels a year in direct healthcare costs by 2025, rising to 110–253 million by 2040**, through type 2 diabetes and ischaemic stroke alone.{% include cite.html id="taub-2024" ex="savings" %} In the United States, the magnesium-water campaigner Paul Mason estimated that the drag of magnesium deficiency on the economy exceeds **$86 billion per year**, a figure reprinted by *Life Extension* in 2005.{% include cite.html id="life-extension-2005" ex="mason" %} Meanwhile, magnesium supplementation costs a few cents per day.

### The vicious cycle of medications

Many commonly prescribed medications **worsen** magnesium deficiency: proton pump inhibitors (PPIs), thiazide and loop diuretics, certain antibiotics, immunosuppressants, chemotherapy agents, corticosteroids, oral contraceptives.{% include cite.html id="kothari-2024" ex="causes" %}{% include cite.html id="schwalfenberg-2017" ex="drugs" %}{% include cite.html id="dinicolantonio-2018" ex="copd" %}{% include cite.html id="workinger-2018" ex="contraceptives" %} Dr Carolyn Dean, a physician and naturopath, describes the pattern she sees:

> *"Many drugs used for magnesium deficiency symptoms cause more magnesium deficiency. For example, here's a common pattern that repeats over and over in the average doctor's office: You experience a stressful event in your life and, as a result, you suffer with headaches, insomnia, achiness, and exhaustion, which are all magnesium-deficiency symptoms. [...] You are prescribed a diuretic, which drains more magnesium from your body. [...] Suddenly you are on two more medications—a statin drug for cholesterol and a drug for diabetes, both of which deplete magnesium!"*{% include cite.html id="dean-interview" ex="scenario" %}

The statin in that scenario is not incidental: magnesium itself acts on HMG-CoA reductase, the enzyme statins block, as a regulator rather than an inhibitor.{% include cite.html id="schwalfenberg-2017" ex="hmg" %}

## What the experts say, in their own words

### Dr Mildred S. Seelig (1920–2004): The pioneer

Professor of nutrition and preventive medicine, Mildred Seelig devoted more than 40 years to documenting the impact of magnesium deficiency. Her 1980 work, *Magnesium Deficiency in the Pathogenesis of Disease*, remains a foundational reference:

> *"Until magnesium clinical methodology is improved and made available, the importance of correcting magnesium deficiency in man's diet and of preventing intensification of a deficit when needs are increased by physiologic or pathologic processes and drugs will have to be inferential-based on experimental and epidemiologic observations."*{% include cite.html id="seelig-1980-preface" ex="inferential" %}

**That warning was issued more than 45 years ago.**

### Prof Jean Durlach (1925–2017): The founder

French neurologist and biochemist, Durlach created the international scientific infrastructure for the study of magnesium. He wrote in 2004:

> *"About 20% of the population consumes less than two-thirds of the RDA for Mg. Women, particularly, have low intakes."*{% include cite.html id="durlach-2004-gestational" ex="abstract" %}

**He issued warnings for more than 45 years. He died in 2017 without the world having acted.**

### Dr James J. DiNicolantonio: The call for urgency

Preventive cardiology researcher at Saint Luke's Mid America Heart Institute, DiNicolantonio classified subclinical magnesium deficiency as a **public health crisis** in 2018:

> *"Because serum magnesium does not reflect intracellular magnesium, the latter making up more than 99% of total body magnesium, most cases of magnesium deficiency are undiagnosed. [...] Subclinical magnesium deficiency increases the risk of numerous types of cardiovascular disease, costs nations around the world an incalculable amount of healthcare costs and suffering, and should be considered a public health crisis."*{% include cite.html id="dinicolantonio-2018" ex="abstract" %}

### Drs Schwalfenberg & Genuis (University of Alberta)

Their 2017 review, *The Importance of Magnesium in Clinical Healthcare*, demonstrates that **Level I evidence** (the highest in evidence-based medicine) supports the use of magnesium in the prevention and treatment of migraine, metabolic syndrome, diabetes, hyperlipidaemia, asthma, premenstrual syndrome, pre-eclampsia, and various arrhythmias.{% include cite.html id="schwalfenberg-2017" ex="levelone" %} Despite this, the knowledge does not translate into clinical practice.

### The MaGNet Network: Reference values are wrong

The international MaGNet network demonstrated that current thresholds (often 0.70–0.75 mmol/L) are **too low** to detect subclinical deficit. Their recommendation, published by Rosanoff and colleagues in the *European Journal of Nutrition* (2022):

> *"Widely varying serum magnesium reference ranges render our use of this important medical tool imprecise, minimizing impacts of low magnesium status or hypomagnesemia as a marker of disease risk."*{% include cite.html id="rosanoff-2022" ex="conclusion,results" %}

Recommendation: set the minimum threshold at **0.85 mmol/L**. In their own survey, 41 of 43 institutional cut-offs were still below it.{% include cite.html id="rosanoff-2022" ex="survey" %}

A century of warnings, a nutrient whose deficit the routine test hides by construction, and a food supply whose magnesium content has fallen against a background of intensifying medical demand: magnesium is a right in the sense [this resource uses the word](/definitions/physiological-rights/).
